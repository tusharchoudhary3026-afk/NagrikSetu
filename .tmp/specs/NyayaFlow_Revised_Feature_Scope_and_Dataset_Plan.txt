1. Case framing and corrections
Case context
The case describes grievances arriving through at least four channels and partially separate workflows, complaints in
English/Hindi/Hinglish, generic status labels, and a simulated finding that 23% of grievances were reassigned at least once.
This does not establish that 23% were misrouted or explain why reassignment occurred.
Selected problem statement
How might municipal grievance teams identify and separate multiple issues within one complaint, recommend
responsible departments with grounded evidence, handle unclear or overlapping ownership, and maintain an
auditable human-reviewed decision trail?

Evidence labels required in the submission
 CASE EVIDENCE: facts supplied in the hackathon case.
 
 EXTERNAL EVIDENCE: public documents/datasets with citations and limitations.
 
 ASSUMPTION: unverified premise, especially department ownership rules.
 
 UNKNOWN: currently unavailable information, such as actual duplicate rate, reassignment causes or API access.
 
Differentiation: do not position NyayaFlow as just a chatbot, status tracker or plain classifier. The planned value is
compound-issue handling + ownership review + abstain-and-ask + auditable correction + measured evaluation.
Five corrections to preserve
 Never describe the 23% reassignment figure as a 23% misrouting rate.
 
 Do not claim no Indian municipal dataset exists unless a documented search supports it; describe the synthetic dataset as the
 prototype plan.
 Do not claim another platform lacks voice, language or status features without current primary-source evidence.
 
 Do not claim accuracy, fairness, savings or a real pilot before measurement. Report denominators and limitations.
 
 BUILD means planned scope until implemented and tested; MOCK is simulated; ROADMAP is not implemented; REMOVED is
 out of V1.

Dataset construction sequence
 Choose 8–10 department categories and 10–15 issue types from a selected city's public charter/function documents; save
 sources.
 Build responsibility table with owner, overlap, source and verification status; mark uncertain rules ASSUMPTION.
 
 Write 100–200 fictional complaints across English, Hindi and Hinglish; include clear, compound, vague, missing-location,
 sarcasm and unusual spelling cases; add 10–15 safety cases.
 Have a teammate who did not write each complaint label owner(s), sub-issues and ambiguity. Double-label a sample if
 possible.
 Create about 20 true duplicate pairs and 20 look-alike pairs; add synthetic transfer histories with reason codes.
 
 Group related/near-identical records before splitting. Freeze test data before model tuning.
 
 Record actual test results, sample sizes, failures and limitations; never fill results with planned or fabricated numbers.
 
Recommended build order
 1. Synthetic dataset + sourced responsibility table first.
 
 2. Rule-based baseline + evaluation harness; freeze test split.
 
 3. Intake/extraction with original-text preservation.
 
 4. Compound splitter and human confirmation/merge-back.
 
 5. Human review actions, append-only ledger and transfer reason codes.
 
 6. TF-IDF router; add multilingual embeddings only if feasible and comparable.
 
 7. Dashboard: split view, evidence, ownership conflicts and case replay.
 
 8. Ownership conflict/bounce detection; duplicate candidate guard.
 
 9. Safety review rubric and queue.
 
 10. Grounded explanation checks and reassignment analytics.
 
 11. Everything else remains MOCK or ROADMAP unless implemented and tested.

14. Round 1 submission checklist and references
Round 1 is an investigation and planning round; the plan alone is not evidence that a feature has been built.
Required submission artifacts
 Case Investigation / Problem-Solution Brief: case, stakeholders, problem, alternatives, evidence labels, assumptions,
 unknowns, rationale and solution.
 Basic SRS/implementation plan: users, inputs/outputs, functional and non-functional requirements, human control and failure
 handling.
 Genuine AI interaction logs and decision index: prompt/link, model/tool, date, output, what was accepted/changed/rejected,
 verification and reason.
 Acceptance tests AT-01 to AT-06 with result fields completed only after actual runs.
 
 Dataset card: source, licence, language, synthetic/public status, labels, split strategy and known limitations.
 
 Team declarations and attribution for reused data, pretrained models, code and assets.
 
Claim and integrity checks
 Do not turn '23% reassigned at least once' into '23% misrouted'.
 
 Do not claim a dataset, feature or competitor limitation without checking and recording evidence.
 
 Do not claim the system is fair, accurate, faster or cheaper before measuring it.
 
 Do not claim a real staff pilot unless participants actually used the system under a defined procedure; otherwise mark it
 planned.
 Do not call synthetic data representative of a city or its residents.
 
 Mark every simulated chart and every mock integration clearly.
 
References to verify and cite in the final submission
[1] CPGRAMS official portal: https://pgportal.gov.in/ — national grievance-system context, not proof of local municipal features.
[2] NYC Open Data, 311 Service Requests:
https://data.cityofnewyork.us/Social-Services/311-Service-Requests-from-2020-to-Present/erm2-nwe9 — external English-language proxy only.
[3] Open311 GeoReport v2: https://wiki.open311.org/GeoReport_v2/ — category/protocol reference, not local ownership authority.
[4] L3Cube-HingCorpus/HingBERT, LinCE and AI4Bharat IndicXlit/indic-trans — verify official project pages, version and licence before use.
Final rule: separate CASE EVIDENCE, EXTERNAL EVIDENCE, ASSUMPTION and UNKNOWN. Every simulated
chart must say SIMULATED. Every unimplemented feature must remain MOCK or ROADMAP.
